AI · Automation

Good automation gives people time back without hiding the work.

The strongest first automation is usually one repeated handoff that is easy to describe and easy to check.

The strongest first automation is usually one repeated handoff that is easy to describe and easy to check.

Look for a dependable repeat

Start with a process that happens often: capturing a new enquiry, sorting a request, sending a confirmation or reminding a team member to follow up. Write down the trigger, the information needed, the action and the person who checks exceptions.

Keep a human checkpoint

Automation can prepare a response, route an item or update a record. A person should still review cases where context, money or customer trust is at stake. That checkpoint helps teams catch bad inputs and refine the workflow before relying on it more widely.

Measure the practical result: fewer missed handoffs, less duplicate entry or a clearer view of what needs attention. Start small, learn from real use, and expand only when the first step is dependable.
